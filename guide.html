Making your site model

The website cuts a square of ground from the model of the old Sligo borough, the circle one Irish mile around the Lady Erin monument on Market Street, and gives it to you as a 3D print, a laser-cut contour kit or a contour plan.

Step by step

  1. Find your site. Use Street map to find streets by name, then Relief to see the ground. Drag the orange square onto your site, or double-click the map to jump it there.
  2. Size the square. Choose 40 to 500 m. Leave room around your site for context: the street, the river and the buildings opposite.
  3. Look at it in 3D. The view under the map shows the ground and the buildings. Click a building for its number and heights. Heights ×2 or ×3 shows gentle slopes more clearly. The buildings are for viewing only and are never in the downloads.
  4. Choose what to make and the scale. The grey box says how big the model will be, how many print tiles or what paper size it needs, and how many buildings are in the square.
  5. Make it, check it under Your download, and download the files.

The ground model is a 2 m grid. Use it for terrain at 1:500 and smaller. At 1:200 slopes are smooth: steps, kerbs and retaining walls are not in it, so survey those on site.

The three downloads

3D print (terrain only)

  • A watertight 3MF and STL for the Bambu P1S, in millimetres. North is +Y and the origin is the south-west corner.
  • Buildings are not printed. Their footprints are marked on the ground as an incised outline (0.6 mm wide, 0.4 mm deep), a raised outline, a raised pad, or not at all. Model the buildings yourself, at the heights in the table.
  • Roads are cut in 0.3 to 0.8 mm. The river, Lough Gill and the harbour are recessed 0.6 mm below their water level.
  • Squares over 230 mm are split into equal tiles (A, B, C… west to east; 1, 2, 3… north to south). All tiles of one model share one datum, so their edges meet flush.
  • The ZIP also has the numbered plan and the table of building heights.

Laser contour kit

  • One layer per board thickness, nested on sheets. Cut lines are red and engraving blue, in SVG and DXF.
  • Each building's footprint and number are engraved on the layer it stands on.
  • The kit has the contour plan, the table of heights (PDF and CSV) and a README.

Contour plan

  • PDF: the plan on A3, A2, A1 or A0, with contours, footprints, roads, water, spot heights, an ITM grid, a scale bar and a north point. The table of building heights follows on A4 pages.
  • Building footprints can be switched off, and their numbers switched off separately. Buildings are numbered across the square in bands from the north edge, west to east, so neighbours have neighbouring numbers.
  • SVG: the plan, for Illustrator or Inkscape.
  • DXF: for Rhino, AutoCAD, ArchiCAD or Revit.
    • Contours are 3D polylines at their own level (layers CONTOUR and CONTOUR_INDEX).
    • Footprints are at their ground level (FOOTPRINT). Numbers, water, roads and the square each have their own layer.
    • Coordinates are real ITM metres, so the file lines up with other Irish map data. In Rhino, move it to the origin if you need to.
  • Heights CSV: the table, with each building's ITM position.
  • Site pack: load it on the Site Model Hub with Import a site pack, to use the contours, sun and climate, model kit and projection pages for your site.

These come from the OPW 2011 lidar surface instead, capped at 4.5 m for footprints under 60 m² and 10 m for 60–300 m², where the lidar can pick up trees. Sheds without a usable lidar height get 3 m (under 30 m²) or 6 m. Estimated buildings show paler in the 3D view. Check them on site.

Data

  • Ground: the 2006 Sligo survey DTM. Its gaps are filled from the OPW 2011 lidar DTM; the two agree to within 1 cm where they overlap. It is a 2 m grid, levels in m OD. Contains Irish Public Sector Data (OPW) licensed under CC BY 4.0.
  • Buildings and heights: Tailte Éireann, with the estimated heights described above.
  • Roads and water: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). The water levels are from the 1:2500 borough model.
  • Street map: OpenStreetMap tiles. They need an internet connection; the relief map does not.

The same data make the 1:2500 borough model for As Water Arrives, the 16th A-IARG conference at ATU Sligo (March 2027). The contour, laser and 3MF code comes from the Site Model Hub.
